Game genres explained

A genre describes the main type of play a game offers. Knowing the genres helps you describe what you enjoy and find games that match it.

Action

Action and Adventure

These games focus on movement, timing and exploration. Players travel through worlds, solve obstacles and often follow a story. Reflexes matter, but so does curiosity.

Strategy

Strategy and Simulation

Planning is the core skill. You manage resources, build cities or lead armies, and every decision has a longer-term result. Many players enjoy the slower, thoughtful pace.

Role-playing

Role-Playing Games

You guide a character who grows stronger over time. Story, choices and character development are central, and a single game can last dozens of hours.

Puzzle

Puzzle and Logic

Short, focused challenges that reward pattern recognition and patience. Puzzle games are easy to pick up and suit quick sessions on a phone.

Sports

Sports and Racing

Digital versions of football, cricket, basketball or motor racing. They range from realistic simulations to light arcade-style play.

Casual

Casual and Family Games

Simple rules, friendly visuals and low pressure. These games are designed so that anyone in the household can join in without a long learning curve.

How a video game is made

Behind every game is a team that combines several skills. Designers decide the rules and the experience. Programmers turn those rules into working software. Artists create characters, environments and animation, while sound designers and composers build the audio that sets the mood.

Most projects begin with a prototype, a rough playable version used to test whether the central idea is enjoyable. The game is then improved over many rounds of testing, where players give feedback and the team fixes problems before release.

The usual stages

  • Concept: the idea, the audience and the core activity
  • Prototype: a simple version to test the fun
  • Production: art, code, levels and audio are built
  • Testing: bugs are found and balance is adjusted
  • Release and updates: the game is published and supported

Gaming platforms at a glance

The device you play on shapes the games available to you, the controls you use and how much you may spend. Here is a simple comparison.

Mobile

Phones and tablets are the most accessible option. Touch controls suit puzzle, casual and strategy games, and play fits into short breaks.

Console

Dedicated hardware connected to a television. Consoles offer a consistent experience, simple setup and strong support for couch play with friends.

Personal computer

PCs are flexible, support a very wide range of games and allow mouse and keyboard or controller input. Performance depends on the hardware you own.

When choosing a platform, think about where you usually play, who you play with and what you are comfortable spending on devices and games. There is no single best option, only the one that suits your situation.

How to choose a game that suits you

Start with what you already enjoy. If you like stories, look at adventure and role-playing games. If you like thinking things through, try strategy or puzzles. If you prefer relaxed play, casual titles are a good fit.

Check the age rating, read a few independent reviews and look at gameplay footage before you commit. Reviews from several sources give a more balanced picture than a single opinion.

A short checklist

  • Does the genre match your interests?
  • Is the age rating suitable for the player?
  • Does your device meet the requirements?
  • Is it single player, online or both?
  • Does the game include optional in-game purchases?

Healthy gaming habits

Gaming is best enjoyed as one part of a balanced routine. A few simple habits help keep it positive.

Take regular breaks

Stand up, stretch and rest your eyes every hour or so. Short pauses reduce strain on eyes, neck and hands.

Set time limits

Decide in advance how long a session will be. This is especially useful for younger players and on school or work nights.

Mind your sleep

Avoid long sessions late at night. Screens and excitement close to bedtime can make it harder to fall asleep.

Be careful with spending

Understand what in-game purchases are before using them, and set a clear budget. Parents can use device controls to manage spending.

Stay safe online

Do not share personal details with strangers, use strong passwords and report abusive behaviour through the game's tools.

Keep other interests

Exercise, study, family time and hobbies all matter. If gaming starts to crowd these out, it may be time to adjust your routine.

Frequently asked questions

Is Gamevista a game store or a game download site?

No. Gamevista does not sell, host or distribute games. It is a reading resource that explains gaming topics in simple language.

Do I need expensive hardware to enjoy games?

Not at all. Many enjoyable games run on ordinary phones and older computers. Expensive hardware mainly matters for the most graphically demanding titles.

What do age ratings mean?

Ratings are guidance from rating boards about the suitability of a game's content, such as violence or language. They help families choose games appropriate for the player's age.

How much gaming time is reasonable?

It varies with age, schedule and responsibilities. A good sign is that gaming fits alongside sleep, study or work, physical activity and time with other people.

Are free games really free?

Many are free to start but may offer optional purchases or show adverts. Read the game description and settings so you know what to expect.
